Podcast as a Gift: a birthday gift that lasts for years

Looking for an original birthday gift, one that won't end up in a drawer? A podcast as a gift is an hour of filmed and recorded conversation with someone you love: about their life, their dreams and what matters to them.

It is a gift that works twice. Once on the day itself: the experience of sitting in a studio in front of a microphone and cameras, and feeling that your story matters. And a second time over the years: every time you go back to the episode and hear again the voice, the laughter and the words of the one you love, just as they were in that moment.

What is a podcast as a gift?

A podcast as a gift is a personal episode recorded in a professional studio in honor of a loved one. You buy the episode as a gift, and the guest of honor arrives at the studio as the main guest.

Here is how it looks:

  • The interviewer: A parent, partner, friend or grandchild. The person giving the gift sits opposite the guest of honor and asks the questions.
  • The questions: Questions you always wanted to ask but never had time for. About childhood, dreams, life-changing moments and what they learned along the way.
  • The studio: Professional microphones, cameras and lighting that turn an ordinary conversation into an event.
  • The result: An edited episode you can keep, share with the family, upload to YouTube and come back to every year.

Why it is so moving: what science says

Anyone who has ever sat in a studio in front of a microphone knows the heart beats faster. The lights, the cameras and the feeling that the whole moment belongs to you create an excitement that is hard to replicate anywhere else. And research explains part of it.

Talking about ourselves activates the brain's reward system

In a Harvard study published in PNAS in 2012 (opens in a new tab), researchers Diana Tamir and Jason Mitchell scanned people's brains while they talked about themselves. The findings:

  • Talking about ourselves activates the mesolimbic dopamine system, the same reward systems that respond to tasty food or money.
  • Participants were willing to give up money just to talk about themselves.
  • In an ordinary conversation, about 30% to 40% of what people say is about themselves.

A podcast as a gift does exactly that, only magnified: a whole hour in which a person is asked about their life, by someone who loves them, and their story is documented with professional cameras. That is why guests of honor leave the studio smiling.

An experiential gift brings people closer than a material one

A study by Cindy Chan and Cassie Mogilner, published in the Journal of Consumer Research in 2017 (opens in a new tab), found that experiential gifts strengthen the bond between giver and receiver more than material gifts. The reason: an experience evokes a stronger emotion at the moment it is lived.

A podcast as a gift combines both worlds: it is a shared experience on the day itself, and also a keepsake you can hold and return to.

A gift for every age

A podcast as a gift suits almost every person and every occasion. What changes is the questions.

A podcast for kids: documentation for years to come

A gift for a birthday, a bar mitzvah or a bat mitzvah. The child tells who they are now, what they love and what they dream of becoming. In ten years, this episode will be a treasure.

And there is another reason. Researchers Marshall Duke and Robyn Fivush of Emory University found that children who know their family stories show higher self-esteem and lower anxiety (the Do You Know scale (opens in a new tab)). A filmed conversation with a parent is a wonderful way to pass those stories on.

A podcast for friends: your story

How you met, what you went through together, and what you love about each other. A perfect gift for a milestone birthday, an anniversary or a friend moving abroad.

A podcast for seniors: a life story

Grandparents tell about their childhood, immigrating to Israel, first love and the advice they want to leave to their grandchildren. It preserves a family heritage, in the narrator's own voice.

Here too the research is supportive. A meta-analysis published in Frontiers in Psychiatry in 2023 (opens in a new tab) examined 27 studies with 1,755 older adults. It found that structured conversations about memories from the past (reminiscence) reduced symptoms of depression and improved life satisfaction. One episode in a studio is not therapy, but it invites exactly this kind of conversation: someone who listens, asks and wants to hear.

A gift that lasts for years: the voice of a loved one

Most birthday gifts are forgotten within a few months. A podcast as a gift goes the opposite way: the more years pass, the more it is worth.

Photos preserve the face. A podcast preserves what a photo cannot: the voice, the way of speaking, the laughter and the thoughts.

The voice of a close person has a special power. In a 2010 study from the University of Wisconsin (ScienceDaily (opens in a new tab)), girls aged 7 to 12 went through a stressful task. Those who heard their mothers' voices on the phone showed a rise in oxytocin, the bonding hormone, and a drop in cortisol, the stress hormone. The response was almost identical to that of girls who got a hug from their mothers face to face.

Now imagine a whole hour of that voice, filmed and recorded in high quality, available on YouTube at any moment. The child who will grow up and hear themselves at eight. The friends who will return to the episode in a decade. The grandchildren who will hear grandma tell her story in her own words. It is a gift for generations.

Frequently asked questions about a podcast as a gift

Who is a podcast as a gift suitable for?
For every age: children on a birthday or bar mitzvah, friends and partners, and parents and grandparents in their senior years.
Who asks the questions?
Usually the person giving the gift: a parent, partner, friend or grandchild. That is part of the magic: the conversation is between two people who love each other.
Do you need experience in front of a camera?
No. We prepare the questions in advance and guide you in the studio. After a few minutes you forget the cameras are there.
Do you have to publish the episode?
No. You can upload it to YouTube publicly, share it by private link only with the family, or keep the file to yourselves.
What do you get at the end?
A filmed and edited episode with professional sound, ready to watch and share. Package details appear on the pricing page.
