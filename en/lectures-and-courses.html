Talks and online courses: how a business owner becomes a leading brand in their field

The fastest way to go from just another supplier in the market to the market leader is to teach what you know. Someone who lectures on a subject is seen as understanding it better than anyone, and someone who built an online course turned their knowledge into a product.

Talks and online courses do three things at once: they build a strong personal brand, they bring in money on their own, and they bring you closer to your customers. In this article we look at what research says about each, and how to get started.

Personal branding: people buy from people

Personal branding is the way the market knows the person behind the business. It is hard to love a logo and a company name. A person who explains, teaches and shares from their own experience builds a real connection.

The research backs this up. In the Edelman-LinkedIn 2025 (opens in a new tab) report on thought leadership, it was found that:

  • 64% of decision-makers trust professional content from experts more than marketing materials.
  • 71% say such content proves a vendor's value better than traditional marketing.
  • 65% prefer a human, informal tone over an academic voice.

A talk and a course are exactly this combination: professional knowledge in a human voice. They don't say "buy from me". They show why it is worth buying from you.

And there is another advantage: a personal brand stays with you. If you change fields, expand the business or change the product, the audience that trusts you will move with you.

The lecturer as market leader: who teaches, leads

In every field there are dozens of service providers, and only a few of them stand in front of an audience and explain the field itself. Those few are the ones the market remembers, recommends and invites to conferences.

According to the same report by Edelman and LinkedIn (opens in a new tab), 95% of "hidden" decision-makers (those who influence a purchase behind the scenes) say high-quality professional content makes them more open to sales approaches. 79% of them say they would prefer to advance a proposal from a company that publishes such content consistently.

A talk does three things an advertisement cannot:

  • Marks you as an authority. Someone invited to explain is seen as someone who knows.
  • Proves instead of promising. The audience hears your method in action, not just reads about it.
  • Lasts over time. A recorded talk keeps working on YouTube and on the website years after it was made.

This is how a market-leader position is built: not with a slogan, but with repeated proof of knowledge.

A live talk on social media: the stage every business owner already holds

A talk used to require a hall, a producer and an audience that shows up physically. Today a live talk on YouTube, Facebook or Instagram reaches your followers directly, and everyone they share it with.

The audience is already there. According to the annual survey by Wyzowl for 2026 (opens in a new tab), 96% of people have watched an explainer video to learn about a product or service, and 84% want to see more video from brands.

What a live talk gives that a regular video doesn't:

  • A real-time conversation. Viewers ask in the comments, and you answer them by name. It is the closest experience to a personal meeting.
  • A sense of occasion. A date set in advance makes people set time aside, sign up and show up.
  • Authenticity. In a live broadcast you can't hide behind editing. Someone who answers open questions with confidence proves real expertise.
  • Content afterward. The recording stays on the channel, and you can cut short clips from it for weeks of publishing.

A regular monthly talk turns your page from a business page into a community waiting for the next meeting.

An online course: your knowledge becomes a source of income

An online course is a product built once and sold again and again. It is not limited by your working hours, so it lets the business grow without doubling the workload.

The market is huge and keeps growing:

  • The global online-learning market was estimated at about $204 billion in 2025, with a forecast of about $279 billion in 2029 (Statista, as quoted in Ruzuku (opens in a new tab)).
  • Creators on the Kajabi platform alone earned more than $9 billion from courses, communities and digital products, as of April 2025 (BusinessWire (opens in a new tab)).

The data from Kajabi's 2025 report is even more interesting. Creators' income from platform payments fell by 33%, and from brand deals by 52%. By contrast, creators who sell their own products, such as courses, earned 25% more on average. 55% of them said that owning the audience is the most important success factor.

The conclusion for a business owner: your own knowledge product, sold to your own audience, is more stable than any algorithm.

An online course can serve in several roles:

  • A standalone product for those not yet ready for the full service.
  • An entry door that leads to personal coaching or a more expensive service.
  • Part of the service that saves you from explaining the same things to every customer again.

Someone who teaches their customers understands them better

Talks and courses are not just a marketing channel. They are also the best market research a business owner can do.

  • The questions reveal the pain. Every question in a live talk tells you what your customers don't understand, what they fear and what stops them from buying.
  • The course shows where people get stuck. A lesson many people leave halfway, or a chapter they return to again and again, are an exact map of the market's needs.
  • Preparation sharpens expertise. In a study published in Memory & Cognition in 2014 (opens in a new tab), participants who expected to teach others remembered the material more completely and in a more organized way, and understood the main ideas better. This is called the "Protégé Effect". Someone who builds a course reorganizes their knowledge, and comes out a better expert.
  • The relationship becomes personal. A student who has gone through a whole course with you knows your voice, method and values. They are the business's natural ambassador.

This creates a self-feeding circle: you teach, hear the customers, improve the service, and teach again at a higher level.

How to start: 5 steps to your first talk and first course

  1. Choose one customer problem. Not "everything about real estate", but "how to check a rental contract before signing". A focused topic attracts a precise audience.
  2. Start with a live talk. It is the fastest test: the audience's questions will reveal what truly interests them.
  3. Build a course from the talks. Collect the topics that worked, arrange them in a logical path, and add an exercise or worksheet to each lesson.
  4. Emphasize production quality. Sound and picture affect credibility. A 2018 study found that poor sound made audiences judge lecturers as less intelligent and the content as less important (ScienceDaily (opens in a new tab)). Someone selling knowledge cannot afford to sound like an amateur.
  5. Make it routine. One regular talk a month matters more than ten talks at once. The audience learns to expect you.

Frequently asked questions about talks and online courses

What is personal branding?
Personal branding is building a business owner's reputation as an expert in their field. The market knows the person, trusts them, and through them trusts the business too.
Is an online course suitable for every business?
For almost every knowledge-based business: consultants, therapists, lawyers, coaches, real-estate professionals and professional service providers. Even when the course doesn't sell, it can serve as a marketing tool or as part of the service.
Which is better: a live talk or a recorded course?
They complement each other. A live talk builds an audience and a connection in real time. A recorded course turns knowledge into a product that sells without a time limit.
Which platform should I broadcast a talk on?
Where your audience is. YouTube suits content that stays and can be found in search, Facebook suits communities and groups, and Instagram suits a personal, casual connection. You can broadcast to several platforms at once.
How long does it take to build a personal brand?
It is a cumulative process. Consistency matters more than intensity: a monthly talk for a year will do more than one big campaign.
